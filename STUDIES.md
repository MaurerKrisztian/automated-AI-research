## Key results

- **0001 · Prefix-Cholesky: exact chunkwise least-squares memory** (review, E3): One small Cholesky per chunk gives every token its exact least-squares memory readout; with a carried inverse and closed-form backward, a training pass took 21.5 ms vs 68.7 ms for the first version and 621.4 ms for 30 CG steps differentiated by autograd (RTX 5060 Ti, float32).
- **0002 · Exact chunkwise MesaNet: forget gates as a per-token ridge** (review, E2): Forget gates act as a per-token ridge, so one eigendecomposition per chunk plus small Woodbury solves gives exact readouts and closed-form gradients (float64 error ≤ 4.5e-14 when well-conditioned); on an RTX 5060 Ti, with a batched Triton Jacobi eigh, the forward solve is 2.1–8.8× slower than 30-step CG but faster than CG at matched error in 9 of 12 ill-conditioned cells.
- **0003 · Chunk-parallel Kalman memory: one Cholesky per chunk** (review, E3): For the Bayesian Layer's Kalman memory, one C×C Cholesky per chunk gives every exact readout and gradient (float64, E2), less accurately than the token-by-token filter when cond(𝒦) is large. On an RTX 5060 Ti (E3) its plain-PyTorch forward pass is mostly not faster than BL's algorithm with Triton loops: at each one's best chunk size, BL takes 0.66–1.08× its time eager, 0.68–0.99× as CUDA graphs.
- **0004 · Does the Kalman memory's forgetting train to better recall?** (review, E4): At 2 layers, d_model 64 and 3000 steps (E4), no Kalman variant beat both MesaNet with 30 CG steps and Gated DeltaNet on MQAR, Volatile MQAR or selective copying once the one flagged cell was rerun on fresh seeds. MesaNet leads MQAR at N = 128 with 81.9% (79.8–83.1) against 72.4% (69.9–75.8) for the best Kalman variant, at lower cost per step (8.1 against 13.6 ms). The Kalman line stops.
